# Crisp-drawn OSD banner on macOS 26

Status: approved design, pre-implementation.
Date: 2026-09-02
Issue: #76

## Purpose

Crisp shows an on-screen display for every brightness or volume key press it routes to a display, through the private OSDUIHelper XPC service. On macOS 26 that service draws the pre-Tahoe bezel: a 200 by 200 point square at the bottom centre of the screen with a sixteen-chiclet bar. macOS 26 itself draws a compact glass capsule under the menu bar at the top right of the screen, with the device name and a slider. Red-Pumpkin20 asked on #76 (2026-09-01) for the modern look. MonitorControl gave up on this (their discussions 1799 and 1873: the Tahoe banner is unreachable for third parties, and on 26.0 the helper call landed in the new capsule with an empty bar). BetterDisplay draws its own capsule. Verified on this Mac (26.5.1, Dell U4919DW, 2026-09-02): the helper window belongs to OSDUIHelper at level 2005; the native capsule is a Control Center window at the same level, 352 by 152 points including transparent margins, the visible capsule about 280 by 54 points, 20 points in from the right edge and 12 below the menu bar.

Decision (Didrik, 2026-09-02): on macOS 26 and later Crisp draws its own banner in the Tahoe style. macOS 14 and 15 keep the helper call, whose bezel still matches the system there. No setting, no classic-style option; Crisp stays lightweight.

## Scope

- The triggers do not change. The three key paths in BrightnessKeyService (under-cursor brightness, all-displays and selected-displays brightness, DDC volume and mute) keep calling `BrightnessHUDService.show(level:image:on:)`.
- On macOS 26 and later that call draws a Crisp banner on the target screen. Below 26 it runs the existing OSDUIHelper code unchanged.
- No preference, no new user-facing string. The label is `NSScreen.localizedName`, for volume as well: the native capsule names the audio device, and the display is the audio device in that path.
- Interactive while the pointer is on it, as the native capsule is: it holds itself up, grows a knob that sets the level, and takes a click on a close badge. See Hover below.
- No OSD for brightness changes from other sources (panel sliders, presets, crispctl, auto brightness). Same as today.

## Look

A Liquid Glass capsule, `NSGlassEffectView` regular style, corner radius half the height, 280 by 54 points. Placed on the target screen with its right edge 20 points in from the screen's right edge and its top 12 points below the visible frame's top, so it sits under the menu bar, or 12 points below the screen edge when the menu bar is hidden.

Inside, top to bottom: the display name in a small secondary label, then a level track with a symbol at each end. Brightness: `sun.min.fill` and `sun.max.fill`. Volume: `speaker.fill` and `speaker.wave.3.fill`. Mute: `speaker.slash.fill` on the left, an empty track, no trailing symbol. The track is a thin capsule in the secondary label colour with a primary-colour fill sized by the level. Text and symbols use the primary and secondary label colours so the glass adapts to light and dark on its own. Exact sizes, weights and paddings are tuned live against a screenshot of the native capsule on the same screen.

Window level 2005, the value both OSDUIHelper and Control Center use on 26.5.1, so the banner sits where the system's own does. Collection behaviour joins all Spaces and shows over full-screen apps, like the menu panel.

As built (2026-09-03, measured against the native volume HUD on the Dell U4919DW, both over the same backdrop so the numbers compare): 292 by 64 points, top 10 points below the menu bar, corner radius 20 with a continuous curve, centred on Crisp's menu bar item (see Placement below) or, with no item to measure, 10 points in from the right edge. Measure the native capsule at rest, a second after the key press: it settles down from above through the first half second, and a frame caught during that settle reads 2 points narrower, 1 point shorter and rounder than the capsule the eye sees, which is where the earlier 290 by 63 at radius 26 came from. The capsule is not a glass view. NSGlassEffectView was tried first, on the public clear and regular styles and on all 24 private materials, and none of them can hold the HUD's backdrop: the materials that blur wash the backdrop out well past the HUD, and the ones that do not leave it sharp. A Core Image filter over the glass cannot correct this either: the window server composites the backdrop, not this process, so a filter on a layer above it changes nothing, whatever the radius. So the banner draws its own backdrop: a CABackdropLayer sampling at half resolution with a CAFilter gaussianBlur at radius 1.0 over it, inside a layer masked to the capsule shape. Both numbers are fitted on a page of text, which is what the capsule sits over in use. The bar target the earlier rounds fitted against cannot see this, and that is the first lesson of the round: its narrowest group is four pixels wide and a letter's stem is one, so two capsules can read the same on it and look nothing alike over a real window. The second lesson is the measure itself. Energy above a scale piles every finer band into every number, so a capsule far too soft at the finest detail and about right at the coarsest reads as one flat ratio at every scale, which is what an earlier round read and believed. The measure that separates them is a band decomposition, each band the difference of two box means, taken inside the capsule over the same page behind both. The HUD reads 2.56, 2.65, 3.21, 3.37 and 3.25 at 1 to 2, 2 to 4, 4 to 8, 8 to 16 and 16 to 32 pixels, and the banner 2.33, 2.71, 4.53, 5.36 and 4.76. The radius that shipped before this round, 2.0, read three and a half times softer than the HUD in the finest band, which is what smeared a word the HUD only softens. No one blur holds every band, because the HUD's kernel has a narrower core and a wider skirt than a gaussian has: this one lands the two fine bands and shows about half again as much of the coarse structure, 1.4 is more than twice too soft at the fine end, and 0.6 is not blurred at all. Radius 1.0 is the best of them by a wide margin, a root mean square error of 0.31 in the log of the ratio against 0.72 for the next. The radius is not a smooth dial either: it is quantised inside the filter, and 0.8 measures far sharper than 1.0 where 0.6 measures as no blur at all. Two samples mixed, a sharp one over a heavy one, do fit every band at once, and cannot be built. The glass filter's own five level blur pyramid is inert at every radius, with or without its distances and its face. The layer's own opacity looked like the way in, since what it does not draw is the desktop as it is, but at 0.90 the profile is the same as at 1, band for band, and at the share that would fit, the sharp part carries whole letterforms and the text behind the capsule stays legible. Stacked backdrop layers do not mix at all, because the upper one samples what is already composited below it. An opacity below 1 also reorders the tone filters, and the offset then lands before the multiply and clips a backdrop over 195, which draws a white page 30 levels dark. A measurement note for the next round: the two capsules are not a fixed distance apart, since the banner hangs under Crisp's menu bar item and the item moves, so measure the separation each run and tile the page at it, or the two sample different text. A sparse page is no good either: a bare strip of it falls between the lines, and the HUD's own reading moved by a sixth from run to run until the page was made dense.

The same layer bends its backdrop into the edge, the way an edge of glass does, which is what the HUD does and a plain masked capsule does not: behind the HUD a straight line curves along the inside of the edge, behind a hard-cut capsule it runs straight off. The bend is the system's own glass filter, put on our layer and pointed at a distance-field layer for the shape, which is why it draws nothing on a bare backdrop layer. Its face is left off, since the tone is carried in the same chain, and its own blur is set to zero, since its default is heavy enough to take the fine structure to a third of what the HUD leaves. It was first fitted as displacement rather than by eye: a stripe backdrop of one period behind both capsules, and the phase of that pattern read column by column in from the edge. The HUD pulls its backdrop 4.8 points sideways at the strongest point, 8 points in from the edge, holds a weaker shoulder at 15 points and lets go 26 points in, and -110 over a height of 20 draws 4.4 points at the same place, with the same shoulder and the same release. That measure gets the shape of the bend but not its strength, because it says where the backdrop goes and not how dark it lands, and the tone is what the eye reads: at -110 the banner carried a dark band along the bottom edge over a bright window that the HUD does not have. So the amount is fitted on tone instead, over a backdrop of bands, as how far each column in from the edge sits from the flat tone in the middle of the capsule. The HUD reads 33, 27, 22, 20, 19, 18, 13, 11 and 11 levels off at 2 to 10 points in; -110 reads 35, 30, 27, 22, 20, 19, 17, 14 and 12, a quarter strong the whole way in, and -80 reads 32, 27, 21, 21, 20, 17, 14, 12 and 11, within a level of the HUD at every column. A probe capsule used for this has to sit exactly where the banner sits: an early sweep put its capsule a point further in, read every column one across, and made -90 look like the answer. The height is not a dial: 16 bends nothing at all and 30 folds the backdrop over itself. Well under the fitted amount there is no bend the eye can find at all, which is what the first attempt here (-12 over 8, fitted by eye at 8x) drew. Every class and key on that path is private, so each is asked for by name and any one missing leaves the banner with a blurred backdrop and no bend.

The tone and the colour ride in the same chain, after the blur and before the bend: multiply by 0.657, add 31, saturate by 1.92. The first two are the native tone line, which is linear in the backdrop (out = 0.657 in + 31), and on backdrops of black, mid grey and 233 the banner reads 31, 130 and 184 to the native's 31, 129 and 184. The third is what a grey alone cannot do. Measured on four saturated backdrops the HUD lands on that same brightness line to a tenth of a level and multiplies what is left of the colour by 1.26 every time, where a grey at alpha 0.343 can only keep 0.657 of it, so a coloured window behind the banner stayed visibly duller than behind the HUD; saturating by 1.26 / 0.657 after the line puts it back, and over a strong green the HUD reads 17, 168, 55 and the banner 18, 168, 55. The order matters, because saturating the backdrop before the line drives a strong colour past black in one channel and clips it. Any of those filters missing leaves the banner one flat grey layer instead, white 0.355 at alpha 0.343, which draws the same line without the colour. The content sits above all of it, so the label, glyphs and track keep their own tone, and it is forced to the dark appearance on the hosting view only: the native capsule is dark with white text in either appearance, and reads the same in both, and so does this one. Label 12.25 point regular, 16 points in, on a line box held at 16 points so the track and the glyphs keep their own rows, with its baseline 22.75 points below the capsule top; glyphs 13 point at the same inset; the track 4 points thick, its centre 40.5 points below the top and one point above the glyph centre line, 4 points from each glyph, the unfilled part 7 percent white, with the native's 17 tick dots (2 points, 11 percent white, 6 points below the track centre, 4.5 points in from each end). The label, the glyphs and the track fills are all explicit white, never the primary label colour, which is white at 85 percent in the dark appearance and measures 243 over the same body where the HUD's label measures 251, reading thinner and duller. The dark appearance still goes on the hosting view, for anything semantic left in the content. The label size is measured, not chosen: the same string drawn at 13, 12.5 and 12 points beside the HUD's own label, and each one matched to it by sliding one ink profile over the other at every scale, puts the HUD at 12.33, 12.22 and 12.18 points, and the ascender heights (18.5 pixels at 2x against 19.7, 18.9 and 18.1) agree. The 13 points the banner used before is what read as slightly off next to the HUD without being nameable. Compare like with like here: an ink or stroke width read off two different strings measures the strings, and an AppKit probe measures its own tracking, since NSAttributedString does not apply the optical tracking a SwiftUI Text does. On the same screen the label now sits on the HUD's rows, ascender top to baseline, and the track sits on the same four rows with the same left inset and right end. The native capsule carries a bevel, a rim one point wide along its edge, which the banner draws as a layer border of white at alpha 0.36 on a view over the content, so it follows the corner radius and the entry and exit resize. It reads 112 over a body of 31 on black and 175 over 130 on mid grey to the native's 105 over 31 and 176 over 129. One difference is left. The banner passes the two fine bands of the backdrop about as the HUD does and about half again as much of the coarse, since a gaussian cannot follow the HUD's kernel at both ends, and it carries a little dither of its own (0.25 where the HUD reads 0.02 over a flat backdrop). The rim is not one of them, whatever an earlier note here said: measured as the lift over the capsule's own body, which is the only thing that means anything, the native rim lifts 73, 55, 34 and 24 levels over backdrops of 0, 114, 215 and 247 and the banner's lifts 81, 53, 30 and 21. The reading that said the rim ran hot over a near-white backdrop had compared the banner's rim to the backdrop instead of to the native's rim. Entry, hold and exit are fitted to the system HUD frame by frame, against the tone the screen actually shows: the native capsule takes about half a second to darken along a curve with a slow start while it grows in from 11 points narrower and 2 points shorter on each side and settles down 5.5 points over 0.35 seconds, eased out. It holds 1 second after the last press, then lifts back, shrinks a little further than it grew from (14 by 3 points a side) over 0.45 seconds along a cubic bezier with control points (0.2, 0.4) and (0.3, 1), and fades on its own group: the native exit falls fast and tails off, and a plain ease-out ends 0.1 seconds too early. The fade in runs 0.55 seconds on a bezier with control points (0.4, 0.05) and (0.2, 0.9), the fade out 0.54 seconds on (0.2, 0.65) and (0.35, 1). Both were re-measured after the backdrop layer replaced the glass and both still hold: through the entry the banner tracks the native within a frame or two at every sample, and through the exit the two fall together to a twentieth, where the native keeps a small tail about 80 milliseconds longer. A press during the entry lets it finish. A press during the exit stops the exit where it is and comes back up from there: a second animation on a window property does not replace the one in flight, the two drive the window together and the exit wins, so the banner used to blink out and come back before the press took hold.

The dots mean something, which they did not before. macOS moves brightness and volume between sixteen fixed stops, 6.25 apart, and the HUD's seventeen dots mark them: three steps up its fill ends on the third dot, eleven steps up on the eleventh, to a tenth of a pixel at 2x, and only at the top of the range does it run past the last dot to the end of the track. Two things kept the banner off them. Its keys added or took away a sixteenth of the range from wherever the value happened to be, so a display found at 79 percent went to 85.25, 91.5 and 97.75 and never touched a stop; `Models/BrightnessKeySteps.swift` now returns the next stop above or below instead, which is what the system's own keys do, and the caller still clamps it to the display's maximum so Extra Brightness carries the grid on past 100. And the track drew its fill as a plain fraction of its width with the dots 6 points in, where the HUD insets its dots 4.5 points and ends the fill on the dot for the level. With both fixed, the fill lands on the dot at every step. Two cases are left where it cannot: with Extra Brightness on the track spans 0 to the display's maximum, so a step is half a dot, and on a display whose own brightness is not on the grid, the first press moves it there rather than a whole step.

Accessibility > Display > Reduce transparency changes the system's capsule, and the banner follows it. Measured on 26.5.1 with the setting on, over flat backdrops of 0, 115, 179 and 255, the HUD draws 26, 54, 70 and 89, which is a line of 0.247 in + 26 where it otherwise draws 0.657 in + 31, so the capsule is much darker. Its backdrop keeps no detail at all: 0.09, 0.17, 0.19 and 0.26 of the energy a page of text carries at 2, 4, 8 and 16 pixels, against 1.00, 2.26, 3.15 and 4.23 with the setting off. It still tracks what is behind it, so this is a heavier blur over the same sample and a darker line, not a flat fill, and the same structure draws it: a second scrim colour (white 0.135 at alpha 0.753) through the same algebra, a saturation of 1.65 instead of 1.92, and a blur radius of 20. The banner lands on 26, 54 and 89 on those backdrops and on 27, 75, 39 over a strong green to the HUD's 27, 76, 39. The tone is the same in both appearances, as it is with the setting off. The close badge goes flat: no sample of its own and no rim, the same disc over every backdrop, and it still follows the appearance, 242 with a 122 cross in light and 20 with a 149 cross in dark, where the banner draws 20 and 150. The capsule keeps its rim (101 over a body of 26 on the HUD, 108 on the banner). Nothing watches the setting: every filter is set when a panel's layers are made, so the panels are dropped and built again the first time the banner comes up after the setting moves, which costs nothing while no banner is on screen.

## Placement

The banner hangs under Crisp's own menu bar item, and lights it while it holds, which is what the system does with its own HUDs. Measured on 26.5.1 with two screens: the brightness HUD's capsule spans 1072 to 1364 points on a 1512 point screen, centre 1218, and the Display control's item window is 1200 to 1237, centre 1218.5. The volume HUD looks like a corner banner, right edge 10 points in, and is not: the Sound control sits 123 points from the screen edge, so centring the capsule on it would put its right edge a point past the screen, and 10 points in is where it lands when it cannot centre. So the rule is centre on the owning item, and no closer than 10 points to either side edge, which reproduces both HUDs where they sit.

A full-screen space is the one case where the capsule does not hang under an item at all. The space takes the menu bar, and every item on it, to another screen, so there is nothing left to measure against, and the system centres its capsule on the screen instead: measured on 26.5.1 over a full-screen window on the external, its window and the banner's now land on the same x to the point. A screen whose menu bar has simply moved away is a different case, and there the system keeps the top right corner, which is what the banner does. The signal for the difference is a window covering the display exactly, owned by an app with a Dock tile, measured in both states: nothing covers a display exactly in the ordinary case, and a zoomed window stops short of the menu bar, so it does not match. The owner check is there because an accessory app can cover a display too: the Cua Driver desktop tool keeps an empty overlay over a whole display while it runs, and before the check the banner centred on that screen's midline, 708 pt from its item (2026-09-07). Only an app with a Dock tile takes a space full screen. The vertical placement needs nothing: over a full-screen window both capsules already sat on the same rows. The answer is held for half a second per display, because it costs a round trip to the window server on every key press: usually about 1.3 milliseconds here, but 53 on a bad one with only thirty windows open, which on a held key is a hitch in the fade the press is driving. A space takes far longer than a banner's hold to come and go, so a stale answer inside that window cannot be wrong in practice.

The item is lit the way the system lights the Sound control while its own HUD is up: the same standard button highlight, a rounded background 24 points tall and about four points wider than the item, which reads as the same overlay on the same wallpaper (measured on the menu bar: the system lifts the item by 16, 17 and 19 levels of red, green and blue, and Crisp's by 12, 16 and 20). It goes out as the banner starts to leave, not when it has gone: the system's own item goes dark between 0.85 and 0.95 seconds after the press, with its capsule still half there, and Crisp's now goes dark in the same bracket. The close badge is the exception: the banner goes at once there, so the light goes with it, unless another screen's banner is still up. An open Crisp panel holds the same highlight, so the two are read together and neither turns the other off.

Crisp's item lives on one screen at a time, and the menu bar draws the same items on every screen, so on a screen the item is not on the banner mirrors its offset from the right edge; AppDelegate.positionPanel already places the menu panel that way. Measured in all-displays mode with two screens up at once, each banner's centre lands within two points of its own screen's copy of the icon. A menu bar with no room for the item leaves that item's window away from the bar, which the anchor drops, and the banner keeps the top right corner it had before. The lit item is the real one, so it lights on the screen the item is on, which is not always the screen the banner is on.

## Hover

The capsule takes the pointer the way the system's does. The pointer resting on it holds it up, grows a knob on the track and puts a close badge on the top left corner; dragging the knob sets that display's level as it moves, and the badge sends the banner away. The pointer leaving starts the 1 second hold again.

The track has two states. At rest it is drawn here: a groove, a white fill ending on the tick for the step, and the sixteen dots. While the pointer is on the capsule it is the control Crisp's own panel rows use, a SwiftUI `Slider` at the small control size tinted white, which brings the knob, its growth under the pointer, the glass it turns to while it is held, and the drag itself. The two lines land on the same rows at the same tone, so the swap moves nothing (measured settled on the same backdrop: rows 79 to 82, 255, fill starting at the same column in both).

Why the control is not there at rest as well: AppKit draws a slider in a window that is not key in its inactive state, where the line is a grey darker than its own groove (71 against 82 over a backdrop of 91) and the knob has no glass. Only a key window draws the real one. Nothing else reaches it, all measured: `.tint`, `NSSlider.trackFillColor`, the `controlActiveState` environment value, an overridden `isKeyWindow` and a posted `didBecomeKeyNotification` change nothing, and any `NSSliderCell` drawing override, even one that calls super, drops the control to its old drawing and loses the glass. So the panel takes key while the pointer is on the capsule and hands the keyboard back with `NSApp.deactivate()` when it leaves, unless Crisp was the app in front when the pointer arrived: deactivating then would put Crisp's own window, an update or the About box, behind whatever is next. It cannot hold key while the banner is merely up, or every brightness press would take the keyboard away from whatever is in front. Measured live: with the banner up the keyboard stays with the app in front, while the pointer is on the capsule it is Crisp's, and it is back with that app the moment the pointer leaves, with the frontmost app never changing.

The knob's centre travels the track inset by half its width, which is not the tick grid the resting fill follows: a click a quarter of the way along the native track set its volume to 23, not 25, because what the pointer moves is the knob's travel. While the knob is out the fill stops at its leading edge instead of running under it, which is the native's behaviour too.

The close badge is a disc 18 points across, its centre 6.5 points in from the capsule's top left corner, so it hangs 2.5 points over it. It does not sit on the capsule at all: swept over six flat backdrops the native disc reads 178, 185, 199, 224, 238 and 249 where the desktop reads 56, 79, 116, 169, 211 and 255, a tone line of its own laid over the desktop (0.373 of it plus 157), and no white at any alpha can draw that (0.65 lands on the middle, 0.90 on the top, so over a light desktop a flat badge reads as a grey disc where the native is nearly clear). So the badge is AppKit, on the same private backdrop layer as the capsule, with its own tone filters and a heavier blur: over a checkerboard the native badge leaves 3 levels of the backdrop's 59 where the capsule leaves 15. The two tone numbers are fitted rather than the measured line, because the filters do not realise what they are asked for, and there is a pair of them per system appearance: the native badge is a light disc with a dark cross in light and the mirror of that in dark, over the same desktop, so over one grey backdrop its disc reads 187 against 88. Swept over four backdrops in each appearance, one run per pair on the same screen, its line is 0.431 of the backdrop plus 150 in light and 0.510 plus 23 in dark; ours lands within six levels of it at every backdrop, the difference being a curve in the native line that a multiply and an add cannot draw. The glyph over it is a 9.5 point bold `xmark`, half the disc's way to black in light and 0.55 of its way to white in dark: the native ink measures 0.491 to 0.502 of the way down and 0.551 to 0.555 of the way up over those same backdrops, so it is the disc it goes half way from and not a flat grey. It is centred on its own ink, read off the symbol's bitmap, because SF Symbols carry their own bearings and a hand-measured nudge does not survive a change of size or a screen at one pixel a point. The rim that ends the disc follows the appearance too: white at 0.66 in light, which lands on the 0.485 of the disc's headroom the native rim carries there, and 0.20 in dark, where the native's carries 0.18. Measure any of this on a settled badge, a second after the pointer lands: the badge's own 0.29 second fade moves the disc by up to 14 levels, which is what made an earlier reading of the native line curve where it is straight.

Under the disc is a shadow, which is what makes the badge an object over a light desktop rather than a disc that disappears into it. A layer shadow cannot draw it, since the native reaches more than 20 points past the disc and a CALayer shadow at any radius has faded by then, so it is a radial gradient with its stops read off the native as a drop profile: over a flat backdrop, how far the screen sits below it ring by ring, in the quadrant up and left of the disc where nothing but desktop is behind. Taken that way the native shadow is a black at 0.079 one point outside the disc, 0.044 five points out, 0.024 eleven out and 0.007 twenty-two out, the same profile over two backdrops, and the dark appearance has its own weaker one, 0.048 a point out and gone by twenty. Read it there and not by eye: what made an earlier one read as a drawn circle was the near end, and what made the next one read too wide was a tail a third to a half too strong from eleven points out. What made an earlier one read as a drawn circle was the near end and not the reach: it started a third too strong at the rim and fell away fast, which draws a ring. Every layer made by hand here sets its contentsScale from the window, since AppKit only does that for a view's own layer and the shadow otherwise drew at half resolution on a Retina panel, which banded its ramp into steps. It is masked to the outside of the disc: the disc samples the desktop and lets anything under it through, which took ten levels off its own tone.

The badge and its shadow need room, so the window is 30 points bigger than the capsule on every side and the capsule, its content, the bevel and the hover view all sit inside that margin. They resize with the window, so their margins survive the entry grow and the exit shrink.

Not matched: near the far end of each appearance the native badge sometimes swaps to the other appearance's look, a dark disc in light under about level 35 and a light disc in dark over a backdrop of 166. It is not a fixed threshold, since the same 166 backdrop left the dark look in place on a later run. The swap is a state the system picks from the backdrop it samples, not a tone curve (over a checkerboard the native disc stays flat, and public glass does not swap at all: its disc tracks the backdrop and its ink stays dark at every tone). Reading the backdrop ourselves would need Screen Recording, so ours keeps the look its appearance asks for.

The badge fades in, and it is the only part that does anything on the way in. It comes up on its opacity alone, 0.29 seconds in on an ease-out only a little faster than a straight line and 0.35 out running straight, both fitted to a 75 frames a second recording of the pointer landing on the native HUD; it does not grow and it does not move. The knob and its fill are not animated at all, they are there the frame the pointer is, and their edges sit in the same columns at every step of the badge's fade. Measure this with the banner settled, a good half second after the press: a pointer that lands while the banner is still coming up, or already leaving, reads the banner's own 0.55 second fade instead of the badge's.

Two things about the pointer on a window like this one. SwiftUI's `.onHover` wants a key window and this panel is key only while the pointer is already on it, so the tracking is an `NSTrackingArea` in a plain view over the hosting view, with `hitTest` returning nil so the clicks still reach the content. And `ignoresMouseEvents` is off only while the pointer is on the capsule, because a window takes every click inside it whatever its views hand back, and the window is wider than the capsule: a view that returns nil stops the view below it from seeing the click, not the window below the window, so the margin would swallow clicks on the menu bar over the banner. The tracking area fires whether the window takes clicks or not, which is what makes that safe, and it is also why a hover arriving at a banner already at alpha 0 is refused outright: the window stays where it was and the pointer crossing that corner would otherwise bring back a banner nobody asked for.

## Structure

`Services/OSDBannerService.swift`, `@available(macOS 26.0, *)`, `@MainActor`, a shared singleton:

- `panels: [CGDirectDisplayID: OSDBannerPanel]`, one per screen, created on first use for that screen and never ordered out afterwards. Taking a glass surface off screen replays the materialize bloom on the next order-in (the menu panel note in AppDelegate), so hidden means alpha 0. Every show from hidden plays the entry animation (see As built), which also masks the one-time bloom.
- `show(level:image:on:)`: prune panels whose display is no longer in `NSScreen.screens`, get or create the panel for the screen, update the model, then `reveal(at:)` with the frame recomputed from the screen (resolution changes covered): the entry animation when hidden or fading, a plain move when visible, and the hide timer restarted either way.
- Hide timer: 1 second after the last show, then the 0.45 second shrink with the fade to alpha 0 beside it.
- Key repeat only touches the model and the timer. No allocation per press.
- The panel: borderless, non-activating, `canBecomeKey` (taken only while the pointer is on the capsule, see Hover), `ignoresMouseEvents` while hidden (see Hover), `isOpaque` false, clear background, no AppKit window shadow (the glass edge carries the shape; the edge profile matched the native capsule to within two pixels without one), `animationBehavior` none, `hidesOnDeactivate` false, level 2005, collection behaviour transient, ignores cycle, can join all Spaces, full-screen auxiliary.
- Content: an `NSGlassEffectView` filling the panel, its `contentView` an `NSHostingView` of the banner view.

`Views/OSDBannerView.swift`: SwiftUI, driven by an observable model with title, kind (brightness, volume, mute) and level in 0 to 1 (the service divides the call site's percentage by 100). About 40 lines.

`BrightnessHUDService.show(level:image:on:)`: `if #available(macOS 26.0, *)` forward to the banner service and return, otherwise the existing XPC code. The `OSDImage` enum stays as the shared vocabulary of the call sites.

## Edge cases

- All-displays mode adjusts several screens per press and shows one banner per screen at the same time. Per-screen panels cover it.
- The built-in display shows the Crisp banner in the all-displays and selected modes, since Crisp consumes the key there and macOS draws nothing. In under-cursor mode with the pointer on the built-in, Crisp passes the key through and macOS draws its own capsule; the two never overlap.
- Extra Brightness: the call sites already pass the level as a percentage of the display's extended maximum, so the track shows the position within the extended range, as the chiclets did.
- Screen goes away while a banner is parked: the panel stays at alpha 0 until the next show prunes it.
- A screen without a matching NSScreen never reaches the service; the call sites guard that already.
- The Crisp panel is open. No banner then: the panel shows the same level on a slider a few points away, and the two moving together is noise. `BrightnessHUDService.suppressed` is set while the panel is up, so this holds on 14 and 15 as well. A banner already up when the panel opens goes with the same flag: it draws over the panel, and the pointer landing on it takes key away from the panel, which on a second screen closes it. One still fading out under an open panel refuses the pointer for the same reason.
- A still screen. A layer that samples what is behind it needs the screen composited, and WindowServer stops compositing a screen with nothing changing on it, which leaves the sample empty: the tone line turns an empty sample into level 31 and the capsule reads black until something on screen moves. Holding brightness up at 100 percent is that case, because the level does not move either, so nothing in the banner redraws; on a display driven by gamma the press writes nothing at all, and the decrease key only fixes it because a new gamma table refreshes the whole screen. Every screen capture of it looks correct, since a capture forces a composite, which is why this was found by reasoning and confirmed on Didrik's screen rather than measured. The backdrop layer therefore carries `windowServerAware`, which the system's own glass sets on its backdrop layer, and an animation the eye cannot see (a thousandth of the layer's opacity, taken off as the banner goes) that keeps the layer rendering while the banner is up. EDROverlayManager keeps its overlay alive against the same promotion, by re-presenting at 5 fps. Neither change moves the look: the transfer curve, the tone on three backdrops and the edge displacement all measure the same afterwards.

## Compatibility

- `NSGlassEffectView` exists in the macOS 26 SDK only, and CI builds on that SDK with a macOS 14 deployment target. The service file is gated with `@available(macOS 26.0, *)`, the call with `#available`.
- Both build modes must stay green: `swiftc -swift-version 5` in `make dev` and Swift 6 in Xcode with minimal strict concurrency. Everything in the service is main-actor bound.
- Strict SwiftLint is part of `make check`.
- macOS 14 and 15 keep the `OSDUIHelper` bezel, and that is the right answer there, not a fallback: on those releases the helper is what draws the system's own OSD. Verified on 2026-09-06 in the two tart VMs, 14.8.7 and 15.7.7. In each, a real volume key and the call Crisp makes produce the same bezel, same size, same glyph style, same 16 segment bar, in the same place on the screen. What changed on 26 is that Apple moved its own HUD into Control Center (the capsule window's owner) and left the helper drawing the old bezel, which is the whole of #76.
- One behaviour change reaches 14 and 15: no OSD at all while Crisp's own panel is open, since `BrightnessHUDService.suppressed` guards both paths.
